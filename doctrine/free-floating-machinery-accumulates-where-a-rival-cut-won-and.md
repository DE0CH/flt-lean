## FREE-FLOATING MACHINERY ACCUMULATES WHERE A RIVAL CUT WON — and the disposal test is one question about the STATEMENT
(Same task, and it is what the task was dispatched for.) When two branches close a node
by different routes, the release keeps one. The loser's LEAF is declined — but its
supporting MACHINERY is often kept, because it is sorry-free, genuinely new, and named
in some survey as a missing build. It is then free-floating, which this project
forbids, and it is nobody's job to notice.
Release 31 kept the `formPoint` route to `exists_isCMJInvariant_ne_of_not_equivalent`
and carried `Isogeny.lean`'s 295-line `GaloisTransport` section plus
`MazurCMForm.IsCMJInvariant.map` (Galois stability of CM `j`-invariants) anyway. Total
consumers across the tree: `End.mapRingEquiv` ← `IsCMJInvariant.map` ← **nothing**.
**Before keeping such a block, ask what its statements need that the survivors do not.**
Here the question has a one-line answer that decides it: **do the CURVE'S COEFFICIENTS
MOVE?** The whole section exists because `σ ∈ Gal(ℚ̄/ℚ)` carries a curve over `ℚ̄` to a
DIFFERENT curve `W.map σ`, which mathlib genuinely does not handle. Every other Galois
conjugation in this tree — `exists_cmEndomorphism_classNumberOne`'s `hgal`, the
`Ksep`-descent blocks in `KnownIn1980s/EllipticCurves/` — is along an ALGEBRA map that
FIXES the curve, i.e. mathlib's `Affine.Point.map`, and needs none of it. One grep for
`WeierstrassCurve.map` applied to a RING equivalence separates the two populations.
**The mathematical reason no consumer could be manufactured, and it generalises past
this file: STABILITY IS THE WRONG HALF OF A GALOIS PICTURE.** Stability says an orbit
stays INSIDE a set; every consumer of a "the set is one orbit" fact needs the orbit to
EXHAUST it, which is transitivity. And transitivity is already cheap here without any
conjugation, because `Normal.minpoly_eq_iff_mem_orbit` converts between "same minimal
polynomial" and "same orbit" in one line. So when a leaf could plausibly want
stability, check first whether its real content is transitivity — if it is, the
machinery buys nothing, however much work it was.
**When you do delete, delete the CODE and keep the DIAGNOSIS in place.** A note where
the block stood, naming the sha and line range to recover it (`git show <sha>:<path>`),
what it did, why it went, and the ONE check that would justify restoring it, costs
twenty lines and is the only thing standing between the next agent and rebuilding three
hundred. A deleted block with no note gets rebuilt; a kept block with no consumer gets
re-audited every release.
