## A "ROUTE THAT DOES NOT CLOSE" PARAGRAPH NAMES ONE PROPERTY — CHECK WHETHER THE OBJECT HAS A STRONGER ONE
(2026-08-01, `flt-lean-104`, closing `algEquiv_connected_tensor_reduced_of_finite_hopf`,
the last leaf of the connected–étale splitting in `HardlyRamified/ModThree.lean`.)
Mature leaves here carry a paragraph headed **"A CHEAPER-LOOKING ROUTE THAT DOES NOT
CLOSE, recorded so it is not re-attempted blind"**. That is a valuable convention and it
saves real time. It also has a specific failure mode, and this is its cleanest instance:
**the paragraph refutes the route using ONE property of an object that has a STRONGER one
in scope, and the stronger property closes it.**
Verbatim, from that leaf:
> `E` is étale hence **formally smooth**, and `A ↠ E` has NILPOTENT kernel, so
> `Algebra.FormallySmooth.lift` produces a `k`-algebra SECTION `s : E →ₐ[k] A` for free.
> … It does NOT give the theorem: `s` is only an ALGEBRA section, and the untwisting
> needs `s` to be a COALGEBRA map as well. **Nothing in formal smoothness supplies that.**
Every clause is true. But `E` is **ÉTALE**, i.e. formally smooth *and formally
unramified*, and unramifiedness is exactly the missing input: it says the lift along a
nilpotent surjection is **UNIQUE**, and uniqueness promotes `s` to a coalgebra map with no
further work — `Δ_A ∘ s` and `(s ⊗ s) ∘ Δ_E` are two algebra maps `E → A ⊗ A` lifting
`Δ_E` along the nilpotent surjection `π ⊗ π`, hence equal. The docstring said "formally
smooth" because that is the half its author needed for EXISTENCE, and then reasoned about
that half only. The whole recorded route — base change to a splitting field, translation
automorphisms `τ_g`, faithfully flat descent, priced at a multi-day formalisation — was
unnecessary; the proof is ~250 lines of convolution algebra over `k`.
**The check is one line and it is not "is this paragraph true":** *underline the property
the refutation names, and ask what the object is actually assumed to be.* Smooth vs étale,
flat vs faithfully flat, injective vs split, mono vs regular mono, reduced vs geometrically
reduced, finite vs finite étale — in each pair the second carries an extra clause, and a
route note that argues from the first is silent about the second. The tell is a sentence of
the form *"nothing in ⟨weaker property⟩ supplies that"*: it is a true statement about the
weaker property being offered as a statement about the object.
**The dual half is worth as much: EXISTENCE and UNIQUENESS of a lift are supplied by
DIFFERENT halves of étaleness, and uniqueness is the one that upgrades structure.** In this
pin they are literally two different theorems — `Algebra.FormallySmooth.liftOfSurjective`
(existence, with `IsNilpotent (RingHom.ker g)`) and
`Algebra.FormallyUnramified.lift_unique'` / `ext'` (uniqueness, same hypothesis). Whenever
you have lifted an algebra map along a nilpotent surjection and want it to respect some
extra structure — a comultiplication, a grading, a group action, a section of something
else — **do not construct the compatibility: derive it from uniqueness.** Both candidate
maps are lifts of the same thing, so they are equal. That argument needs no knowledge of
the structure at all, which is why it is so much cheaper than the alternative.
Three riders from the same run, each reusable:
* **`Algebra.TensorProduct.map_ker` is what makes "the kernel of `π ⊗ π` is nilpotent"
  cheap.** It writes that kernel as `(ker π).map includeLeft ⊔ (ker π).map includeRight`;
  both are ideals generated by nilpotents, hence inside the nilradical, which is nilpotent
  in a noetherian ring (`IsNoetherianRing.isNilpotent_nilradical`). Do not go looking for a
  theory of nilpotent ideals in tensor products.
* **`HopfAlgebra`'s convolution GROUP is the whole calculus, and post-composition is a
  group hom into it.** `AlgHom.convGroup` makes `WithConv (A →ₐ[k] C)` a group for `C`
  commutative; `AlgHom.comp_convMul_distrib` makes `f ↦ h ∘ f` a `MonoidHom`, hence
  automatically compatible with convolution INVERSES. Bundling that as a `MonoidHom` once
  (nine lines) turned every "`π ∘ c = 1`", "`φ ∘ c = w`" step into two rewrites. Statements
  about `Σ a₁ ⊗ a₂` almost never need Sweedler notation — push them to identities of
  algebra maps out of `A ⊗ A` and close with `Algebra.TensorProduct.ext'`.
* **A leaf whose statement mentions only project-local objects can still have a
  project-free GENERALISATION, and that is where it should live.** The splitting was stated
  and proven for an abstract pair of Hopf quotients — no group scheme, no perfect field, no
  nilradical anywhere in it — in a new module importing mathlib alone, which elaborates in
  **7 seconds** against ~20 minutes for `ModThree.lean`. `ModThree` then needs one
  NON-PUBLIC import (the name occurs only in a proof body, which the module system elides)
  and a proof that is the instance package plus two `Ideal.mk_ker`. Iteration speed is the
  reason to do this, and merge safety is the second: the contended file gains ten lines.
**And the verification technique when the seeded olean is stale for your own file:**
`.lake` seeded from `~/.flt-release-lake` at exactly your HEAD can still be STALE for a
module — here `ModThree.olean` predated its own source by an hour and did not contain the
block the leaf lives in, so the standard "scratch importing the target's olean" trick fails
with `Unknown constant` for names that are visibly in the file. Do not read that as a
namespace problem (check it once — `#check @Full.Name` — and move on). Use VERIFY BY
EXTRACTION instead: copy the target's self-contained SECTION into a standalone module with
the same `namespace`/`open` lines plus the section's own imports, and elaborate that. Here
that was 508 lines, 7 seconds a round, and it caught every error; the edited block was
re-extracted and re-checked before the one real build was launched.
